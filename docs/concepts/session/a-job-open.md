A layer covers the thread and the composer, which stay mounted under it: Back (the key again, the button, or Esc) returns to the thread at the same offset with focus where it was.
The arrows walk its rows, and focus goes in when it opens, to the search field in Artifacts and to the first row elsewhere.
Artifacts has a List and a Graph, switched by the tabs in its head or by `V` anywhere in the layer but the field. The Graph draws how the Job grew, each thing hanging off what produced it, left to right in the order things first appeared. Its nodes are the messages of yours that something followed, the plan and each of its groups, and one node to each artifact (a link, an issue, a pull request, a sketch, a page, a file, an image, a doc, a child Job). An artifact hangs off the plan group whose task was in progress when its first version was written, else off the message it followed, else off the Job's first message; Fleet works that out and stamps it on each entry of `Session.versions` as `from`. A later version that followed something else draws a thin second line from it, and the joins already known are lines too: an issue to the pull request that closes it, a group to the child Job started from it, the plan to each group. An artifact's versions are a row of marks inside its node, each its registry glyph with a tooltip, and a press on a mark opens that version, so an old sketch version opens the drawing as it was; a press on the node opens the latest. Commands, subagents and background tasks are not on it. Tab enters the graph and the arrows move to the nearest node that way; Enter opens a node; a full stop and a comma step through its marks and Esc comes back to the node. The field dims the nodes it does not match (`artifact-graph`).
Esc is the layer's while one is open, and a sheet over it keeps its own.
The layer is dropped when another Job is opened.
