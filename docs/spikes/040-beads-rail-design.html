The workflow rail on beads

A Job's workflow becomes a molecule of beads: one bead per step, which the agent claims and closes, and one gate between steps, which only Fleet closes, from what it saw. The rail stops guessing the step from pushes, first edits and task order. Those guesses raised 116 of the 225 notices agents disputed. Spike 40 shows bd 1.3.2 runs the feature workflow this way, with two gaps Fleet has to fill: any agent can close a gate, and the pull request gate never fires on red ci. Four choices below are yours before anything is built.

Why the rail is rebuilt

Today Fleet works out which step a Job is on from proxies (crates/fleet/src/rail.rs). When a proxy is wrong, the agent gets a notice telling it to do something it already did or should not do. Agents disputed 225 notices in fleet_disputes. These come from the rail or the walk rules:

This changes one rule in docs/concepts/session/the-rail.md: "the agent's word is never an input". The step the agent claims becomes what Bridge shows it doing, as your brief asked. The gates stay Fleet's, so the agent's word still never moves a Job past anything you or the checks must pass.

What Bridge shows

  • The steps, as now, each with one state: done, being worked (the agent holds it), waiting on you (a human gate, naming what to approve), waiting on ci or the queue, or not reached.
  • What the agent is doing: the step it holds and the plan task it holds, beside the activity line Bridge already shows from its tool calls.
  • A mismatch, such as an agent holding Refine while the plan gate is shut, shown on the step as one glyph with a tooltip. It is the one rail notice the agent gets, once.
  • The same on another Mac, when the molecule is poured into a synced backlog.

On the wire, the single step index (workflow_step) becomes a list of steps with their state and holder. Fleet keeps that list in its own record, updated from what it writes and from the journal, and never runs bd to draw a row.

Refuse or nudge

Today nothing blocks a Job; a skipped step draws one nudge (docs/scope.md, the fourth pain, "partly solved"). The grooming Job passed on advice from Anthropic's article on agent loops: procedure goes in instructions, hard limits go in tools that refuse plainly. bd already refuses to close a step whose gate is shut. It does not refuse a claim, and it does not stop a push.

Recommendation: Armada's step tool refuses to start a step whose gate is shut, and Fleet's hook refuses a push before the plan is approved. Nothing else is refused, and the proxy nudges go.

The cost: a Job that should push early, such as a one-line fix you told it to ship, has to ask you to approve a plan first. Today it would push and draw a nudge.

A repository whose tracker is not beads

Recommendation: Fleet keeps its own beads database for it, under Fleet's data folder, unsynced, so every repository runs the same rail.

The cost: every Mac that runs Armada needs bd installed, which Armada would check at start. The alternative keeps today's proxy rail for those repositories. Then two rails are kept, and the disputed notices stay for every Jira or forge repository.

Workflow files in Kit and repositories

bd reads a formula only as <name>.formula.json under .beads/formulas/; it would not pour .armada/workflows/feature.json. A workflow also carries what bd has no field for: instructions, judge criteria, Checks and evidence.

Recommendation: workflow files stay where they are, in Armada's shape, and Fleet cooks each one into a formula when it pours a Job.

Kit's ~/.armada/workflows/, a repository's .armada/workflows/, the Workflows page and its editor are unchanged. The cooked formula lives in Fleet's data folder, never in a repository. The cost is a translation Fleet owns and has to test. The alternative, formulas as the files themselves, puts Armada's fields in bd's metadata, and the editor and the definition schema are rewritten.

Synced or kept local

A poured molecule is ordinary beads, synced to every Mac and to anyone else on the backlog. That serves the goals you added on 4 Nov: swarming, tracking issues and follow-ups, and working across machines. It also puts each Job's steps and gates in the backlog: Fleet's Issues list would show them today. A wisp stays on this Mac, out of the backlog, and squashes to one digest bead when the Job ends.

Recommendation: pour, and filter molecule, step and gate beads out of the Issues list.

The cost: about 10 beads per Job synced to every machine, and each sync carries them. At the 12 Jobs a day of 4 Nov, that is about 120 beads a day.

How it fits background delivery

The background-delivery Job (cf4801f0) recommends that Fleet's own reading of the pull request wakes a parked Job, and that a beads gate only records the wait. This design follows it: the Merged gate is resolved from that same reading, and red ci, a conflict or a queue drop wakes the Job through its hand-off, not through the gate. Neither design builds the other's part.

How it is proven

Before building, the 225 disputed notices become the eval set: each Job's recorded facts are replayed through the new rail, three times each, and the count of notices that still fire is the measure. A dispute that still fires is either a real notice, or a bug in the new rail.

The order of work

  1. The eval set from fleet_disputes, run against today's rail for a baseline.
  2. Cooking a workflow into a formula, and pouring one per Job behind a setting that is off.
  3. Fleet resolves gates from what it sees, reopens one anybody else closed, and reads the journal.
  4. The step tool for agents, and the refusals you choose.
  5. The step list on the wire and in Bridge, with a walk for you.
  6. The eval run against the new rail, then the setting on for Armada's own repository, then the old rail deleted.

Live Jobs keep today's rail until step 6.

What was not measured

  • Sync cost with molecules in the backlog. Spike 38 measured 12 to 33 seconds a sync at 962 issues; 120 more a day was not tried.
  • Pouring through the owner's real database, its remote or a second Mac.
  • Whether agents follow a step tool reliably. The evals measure Fleet's notices, not agent behaviour.
  • Whether bd swarm fits a plan re-approved with groups moved between waves.
  • The cost of cooking each workflow Armada ships. Only feature was written as a formula.
